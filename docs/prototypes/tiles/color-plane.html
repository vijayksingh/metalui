A colour plane for tiles

Every tile is placed on the plane by what it means, and its colours are read off its position. Angle picks the hue, so each quadrant covers a whole range of hues. Distance from the centre decides how far that hue floods the body: near the centre the body is neutral and only the accent carries colour; out at the rim the whole tile is tinted. Angle picks the hue. Going round the plane runs through the whole wheel once, with no jumps: sky, periwinkle, lilac, rose, coral, tangerine, marigold, butter, pistachio, mint, sea-glass and back to sky. Tiles that mean similar things get neighbouring colours.

Doing is vivid; keeping is quiet. Toward the top, tiles glow like tinted resin. Toward the bottom they turn pale and chalky, like pastel stone: quieter but lighter, never muddy.

Distance decides the flood. Near the centre the body is grey plastic and only the accent carries the hue, like the fader reference. At the rim the body takes the hue and the accent is the same hue, turned up, like the peach reference.

The accent is the rim. A tile's accent part and its lamp always take the hue at full strength, wherever the tile sits. White parts stay bone. So every tile has exactly one colour idea, and no two neighbours share it.
